<Epigraph source="Epictetus, Discourses, 2.18">
  Every habit and faculty is preserved and strengthened by the corresponding
  actions: the habit of walking, by walking.
</Epigraph>

You opened the PR. The tests passed. At standup someone asks why that query hits the database twice, and the sentence that rises to your mouth is too honest for the channel: *I'd have to look again*.

It is not a scandal. It is a symptom. Delivery happened; training did not.

## The tool is not the craft

Nobody serious argues that the hammer atrophies the carpenter. They argue about the apprentice who only sees the finished house and never saws. The copilot is a hammer: it compresses the path from intention to diff. What it does not do — what no tool has done, in any craft — is the **reps** that build taste, reflex, the ability to look at a chunk and feel that the premise is crooked.

Epictetus is pedantic here on purpose. Want to be a wrestler? Wrestle. Want to be a reader? Read. The faculty does not install itself by osmosis or by subscription. If the habit of *thinking the code* is replaced by the habit of *accepting the code*, what strengthens is the second. The first atrophies quietly, because atrophy does not page anyone — it only shows up on the day the model hesitates and you hesitate with it.

I say this as someone who uses the tool all day and has caught himself tab-tab-tab until the file felt like someone else's. The useful shame is not "I used AI". It is "I could not rewrite this tomorrow, from scratch, if I had to".

## Delivery and training are different calendars

The market pays for delivery. Askesis is the training that delivery does not invoice until it invoices everything at once — the interview without autocomplete, the incident outside the distribution the model saw, the system nobody has generated yet. Depth, in the earlier essay on this trail, was choosing what to study. Here it is drier: **study by doing**, even when doing it by hand is slower than Tab.

It is not abstinence. It is periodization. The athlete does not train the race at race pace every day; the developer who only competes (closes the ticket) and never trains (writes, errs, deletes, writes again) gets fast on a course the tool already knows and slow on every other.

<Callout label="in practice">
  One rule, not a manifesto: **the first draft of the hard chunk is yours.**
  Then the model can criticise, complete, suggest names. And once a week, a
  small problem with autocomplete off — not to prove virtue, to avoid
  discovering on the worst day that the muscle is not there. If you cannot
  explain the block out loud, it is not training yet. It is rented delivery.
</Callout>

## What the model cannot carry

Seneca said that reading without digestion is food swallowed whole: it occupies, it does not nourish. Generated code you did not chew is the same meal. It enters the repository. It does not enter you.

What migrates across jobs, stacks, models of the season, is the judgment sharpened on the reps — the same artifact that remains when the code dies. Outsourcing the reps is outsourcing exactly that, and calling it productivity.

The copilot will keep offering the short path. That is not up to you. Whoever still walks the stretch that needs legs — that one is.
